A Three-Title Deal, and What It Actually Pays

Exclusivity is the currency now. A six-month, three-title contract at a mid-size studio reads generous on the page and locks a performer out of the four highest-margin weeks of the year.

By Marla Vance / 13 November 2026 / 2 min read

A three-title deal signed in October by a performer represented by a mid-size agency pays a $14,000 guarantee against 22 percent of net on each title, with a six-month exclusivity window and a first-look on anything the studio greenlights next. Read quickly, it looks like a step up from the per-scene work she was doing.

Read against a calendar, it is a trade. The exclusivity window covers December, which is the four weeks when appearance fees and seasonal promotions pay three to five times the January rate. On last year's numbers, the same performer cleared $9,400 in December from appearances alone.

What the guarantee actually buys

Three titles at a $14,000 floor means the studio is buying about nine shooting days plus the marketing obligations attached to each release, including two interview days and one convention appearance per title. The guarantee is not payment for the work; it is payment for the calendar being unavailable to anyone else.

“The number on the first page is the number they want you to compare. The schedule on page four is the number that decides.”Agent who negotiated the deal

The carve-outs are where deals are won. This one leaves cam work outside the exclusivity, caps the first-look at 45 days rather than 120, reverts unused footage after 18 months, and pays within 30 days of release instead of 90. Each clause was traded against something: the studio gave up the 120-day hold to keep the marketing commitments.

The arithmetic that matters to a performer is not the headline. It is the guarantee plus the released carve-outs minus the appearance income the window closes off. For this deal that is roughly $14,000 plus a retained December that a looser contract would have cost her.

Key takeaways

  • Guarantees pay for exclusivity and calendar control, not for shooting days.
  • A six-month window that spans December can cost more than the guarantee adds.
  • The clauses worth trading for: cam carve-out, shorter first-look, footage reversion, 30-day payment.
