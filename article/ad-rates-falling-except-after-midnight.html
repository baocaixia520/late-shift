Ad Rates Are Falling Everywhere Except After Midnight

Display CPMs are down 18 percent year on year across the trade press. The late-night inventory that advertisers used to avoid is the only slot holding its price.

By D. Okonkwo / 16 October 2026 / 2 min read

Display CPMs across the adult trade press are down about 18 percent year on year, with the steepest falls in programmatic remnant inventory. Direct-sold placements moved far less, and in two cases up, because the buyers are not buying impressions; they are buying a slot next to a specific kind of story.

The midnight inventory — pages read between 1am and 5am local time — is the exception. It is only 8 percent of traffic and it carries a 40 percent premium, because the audience at that hour is small, sober enough to read, and disproportionately made up of people who run the businesses being advertised.

Why the premium survives

Advertisers buy attention from operators, not consumers, and operators read at the end of a shift. A banner placed against a story about payment reserves performs better at 3am than at 3pm, and two media buyers said they now ask for the hour-by-hour breakdown before they ask for the rate.

“The inventory is not better. The reader is further down the funnel.”Media buyer at a trade agency

The decline is structural elsewhere. Programmatic buyers have consolidated, the long tail of ad networks has thinned, and the remaining demand is concentrated in a handful of categories — payment processors, hosting, platforms, novelty brands. A publisher that sells 60 percent of its inventory direct is weathering this; one that sells through a network is not.

The publishers holding rates have also cut supply. Two sites reduced display slots from six per page to three, and one dropped its newsletter sponsorship from four per issue to one. Fewer impressions at a higher price has been, for them, a flat-to-up revenue line.

Key takeaways

  • Display CPMs are down about 18 percent overall; direct-sold placements held or rose.
  • Late-night inventory is 8 percent of traffic and commands a 40 percent premium.
  • Cutting slot count has protected revenue better than chasing network fill.
