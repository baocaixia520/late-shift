Interactive Fiction Is Eating the Visual Novel Market

A text engine costs a fraction of a rendered scene and ships in weeks. Studios that moved to illustrated branching stories are releasing four times as often.

By June Alcaraz / 3 November 2026 / 2 min read

A fully rendered visual novel costs $60,000 to $250,000 depending on art, voice and animation. An illustrated branching story with a text engine costs $8,000 to $30,000, and the difference is not the script. It is that a rendered scene has to be built once per branch while a text passage can be written in an afternoon.

Three studios that used to ship one title a year now ship three or four. Their revenue per title is lower, by 20 to 35 percent, and their total revenue is up, because a smaller title can be marketed to a niche and priced at $6 instead of $20.

Where the savings come from

Art: 40 to 90 illustrations instead of 400 rendered frames. Voice: optional, and often the first thing dropped. Localisation: text is cheaper to translate than recorded audio, which makes a second-language release viable at a fraction of the cost.

“We stopped building scenes and started building branches. The branch is the product.”Studio lead who moved from rendered to illustrated

The market is responding. Two storefronts added an interactive-fiction category this year, and a festival that used to have one visual-novel track now has two. The audiences overlap less than expected: illustrated titles skew toward readers who play on a laptop, rendered titles toward players who expect a gallery.

The risk is sameness. A text engine makes it cheap to produce a story and equally cheap to produce one that looks like every other release, and the category is already filling with titles that share an engine, a font and a tone. The studios holding attention are investing in the branch structure, not the art.

Key takeaways

  • Rendered visual novels cost $60,000–$250,000; illustrated branching stories cost $8,000–$30,000.
  • Three studios now ship three to four titles a year at a lower price and higher total revenue.
  • Text is cheaper to localise than recorded audio, which makes second-language releases viable.
