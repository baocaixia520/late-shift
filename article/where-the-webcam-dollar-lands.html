Where the Webcam Dollar Actually Lands

A $10.57 billion market, and the splits that decide who keeps what. On a $100 spend, the model sees between $30 and $50 before tax — and the gap is where studios live or die.

By D. Okonkwo / 11 November 2026 / 2 min read

A viewer spends $100 on tokens. Before anyone performs, three parties have taken a cut: the processor, the platform and, in a studio arrangement, the studio. What remains is split between the performer and whoever paid to bring the viewer in.

The processor takes 3 to 5 percent, plus a chargeback reserve that can reach 10 percent of monthly volume and is held for 90 to 180 days. The platform keeps 30 to 50 percent of the token value depending on the contract and the traffic source, and a studio with its own billing arrangement keeps 20 to 40 percent of the platform's share. Affiliates take 10 to 20 percent when the viewer arrived through a link.

The four-line invoice

Studios that model this properly use a four-line invoice: gross token sales, platform share, studio share, net to performer. Everything else — reserves, chargebacks, affiliate payouts, the 1099 at the end of the year — sits inside one of those lines, and the argument is always about which.

“Everyone in this business can quote their percentage. Almost nobody can quote their reserve.”Payments consultant who audits studio statements

The reserve is the line that breaks small studios. A 10 percent rolling reserve on $40,000 a month is $4,000 that exists on paper and not in the bank, and a studio that pays performers weekly out of operating cash can run short in the third month without doing anything wrong.

Geography changes the arithmetic. European processors price higher and hold longer; US acquirers are cheaper but underwrite harder; a studio that takes local payment methods in five countries runs five reconciliations. Two of the studios interviewed now price their splits by payment method rather than by platform, which is unusual and, by their numbers, correct.

Key takeaways

  • On a $100 spend, the model typically nets $30–$50 before tax, with the platform taking 30–50 percent.
  • A 10 percent rolling reserve on $40,000 a month ties up $4,000 for up to 180 days.
  • Studios that price splits by payment method, not platform, model their real margin more accurately.
