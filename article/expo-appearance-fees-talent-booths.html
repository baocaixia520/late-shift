Expo Appearances: Who Pays for the Booth, and Who Gets Paid

Appearance fees at trade expos run $2,000 to $12,000 for three days. The number that decides whether it is worth taking is not the fee — it is travel, days off the schedule and who owns the exclusivity clause.

By Marla Vance / 8 November 2026 / 2 min read

Appearance work has rebuilt itself around the trade calendar. A three-day expo booking now pays between $2,000 for a first-time signee at a shared booth and $12,000 for a name that a sponsor will put on the signage, and the spread is set by draw rather than by tenure.

The fee is the easy part to negotiate. Travel is where the margin disappears: a European booking usually means two travel days, a hotel minimum of three nights set by the organiser, ground transport and a per-diem, which for a mid-tier booking can consume 30 to 45 percent of the fee before tax.

The clauses that cause trouble

Three appear in almost every expo contract. A radius clause that stops the performer appearing at a competing booth within 30 days. A photography clause that assigns booth images to the sponsor in perpetuity. And a cancellation clause that pays 50 percent if the organiser cancels and nothing if the performer does.

“We lost a four-figure booking because of a radius clause nobody read until the next expo.”Booking manager at a two-person agency

Cross-border withholding is the second quiet cost. A performer paid by a European organiser without a tax residence certificate can lose 20 to 30 percent at source and wait a year for a refund. Two agencies now take the withholding out of the performer's gross and disclose it on the booking sheet, which avoids the argument later.

The studios care about the schedule, not the fee. A contracted performer appearing at a booth is usually on studio time if the appearance promotes a studio title, which means the studio takes 15 to 25 percent and the booking counts against the marketing obligation in the contract. Performers who are not under contract keep the whole fee and pay their own travel.

Key takeaways

  • Fees run $2,000–$12,000 for three days and are set by draw, not tenure.
  • Travel and organiser hotel minimums can eat 30–45 percent of the fee before tax.
  • Radius, photography and cancellation clauses cause more disputes than the money.
