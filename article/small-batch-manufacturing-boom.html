Small-Batch Manufacturing Is Eating the Catalog

Minimum order quantities fell from 2,000 units to 300 at three factories this year. The catalogue is getting longer, the runs are getting shorter, and the margin is somewhere in between.

By June Alcaraz / 12 October 2026 / 2 min read

Three contract factories that serve novelty brands have cut their minimum order quantities this year, from 2,000 units to 300, in exchange for a 15 to 22 percent unit-price premium. The brands took the deal, because it converts a $40,000 inventory decision into a $6,000 test.

The premium is real and it changes the catalogue. A brand running 12 launches a year at 2,000 units carries $480,000 of inventory at cost. The same brand running 20 launches at 300 units carries $180,000 and learns faster, because a failure costs a third of what it used to.

Where the saving disappears

Setup cost per run does not shrink with the run. Plates, tooling, packaging film and the first-article inspection cost the same whether the run is 300 or 2,000, which is exactly why the unit price rises. A brand that runs 20 short runs pays 20 setups, and the annual setup bill can exceed the inventory saving.

“Small batches do not reduce cost. They reduce the size of the mistake.”Factory owner in the novelty supply chain

The brands making this work have consolidated packaging across SKUs — one box format, one label size, variable printing — so the setup cost is amortised over several products instead of one. Two brands now run four products per production week on the same line, which is the only way the arithmetic clears.

The effect on the catalogue is visible. Ranges are longer, colours and variants multiply, and the same brand that used to ship six products in three colours now ships six products in nine. Retail buyers describe this as more choice and less certainty, which is a fair summary of a 300-unit world.

Key takeaways

  • Minimum order quantities fell from 2,000 to 300 units for a 15–22 percent unit-price premium.
  • A $40,000 inventory decision becomes a $6,000 test, which is the real gain.
  • Setup cost does not shrink with the run; brands that share packaging across SKUs make the maths work.
