Seven Things Buyers Find in Diligence

Every seller says the books are clean. Here is what the buyer's accountant finds in week three, and which findings actually move the price.

By Marla Vance / 20 October 2026 / 2 min read

Diligence on a studio or platform is a two-week exercise that produces a list of findings, of which three usually matter. The rest are negotiating points dressed as risks.

The findings that move price: revenue concentrated in one platform, a payment reserve that is larger than disclosed, and performer contracts that are either unassignable or expire inside twelve months. Each of those is a discount, not a deal-breaker, and each is discoverable from documents the seller already has.

The four findings that do not move price

A content library with unclear chain of title, a marketing spend that cannot be attributed, a founder who is the only person who knows the billing system, and a support inbox with no SLA. Buyers price these as integration costs, typically $50,000 to $150,000, and they come out of the earn-out rather than the headline number.

“The seller always knows about the concentration. The surprise is the reserve.”Accountant who has run eleven deals in this sector

The reserve surprises people because it does not appear on a profit-and-loss statement as a cost. It appears as a cash position that is lower than the revenue implies, and a buyer who reconciles platform payouts to bank deposits sees it in an afternoon.

Contracts are the other recurring problem. A performer agreement that cannot be assigned to the buyer is worth nothing on closing day, and a book of 30 performers on twelve-month terms is, in valuation terms, a book of 30 performers who will renegotiate next year — which is a different business than the one being sold.

Key takeaways

  • Three findings move the price: platform concentration, undisclosed reserves and unassignable performer contracts.
  • Chain-of-title gaps and key-person risk are priced as integration costs, usually $50,000–$150,000.
  • A reserve never appears as a cost, which is why buyers find it and sellers forget it.
