What a Trade Floor Actually Costs

A 10x10 booth runs $4,000 to $15,000 before anybody stands in it. The shows that survive are selling meetings, not square metres.

By Marla Vance / 9 October 2026 / 2 min read

A 10x10 booth at a mid-size trade show costs $4,000 to $15,000 depending on the hall, the corner and whether carpet is included. Staff travel, freight and the two days of lost production add another $6,000 to $20,000. The exhibitor has spent real money before the first badge is scanned.

Organisers are responding by selling meetings instead of floor space. Two shows now bundle a matchmaking schedule with the booth, and one charges $300 for a guaranteed 15-minute slot with a named buyer. The exhibitors who renewed say the meetings, not the footfall, are the line that justified the cheque.

Leads per thousand dollars

The metric that matters is cost per qualified lead, not cost per badge scan. A show that delivers 400 scans and 20 conversations is worse value than one that delivers 90 scans and 25 conversations, even at the same price, and most exhibitors do not measure the difference until the second year.

“We stopped counting badges when we realised we were paying $40 per scan and $900 per deal.”Sales director at a payment processor

The shows with the healthiest balance sheets have narrowed their exhibitor mix. A floor where half the booths sell traffic and the other half sell compliance produces fewer meetings than a floor organised around one buyer type, which is why three events now run separate days for studios, tech and retail.

For a first-time exhibitor the arithmetic is unforgiving: a single show typically pays back only if it produces three to five meetings that turn into contracts inside six months. Two brands that exhibited for the first time this year said they would return only if the organiser could name the buyers attending before they paid.

Key takeaways

  • A 10x10 booth costs $4,000–$15,000 before travel, freight and lost production.
  • Cost per qualified lead, not cost per scan, is the number that decides renewal.
  • Shows are splitting floors by buyer type because mixed floors produce fewer meetings.
