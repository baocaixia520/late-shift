Streaming Stacks Got Cheap. Studio Economics Did Not.

Bandwidth costs fell 40 percent in three years. Total delivery spend at the studios we surveyed rose anyway, because the bill moved to the parts nobody modelled.

By Tobias Lund / 9 November 2026 / 2 min read

Transcoding and egress pricing has fallen roughly 40 percent since 2023, and every studio we spoke to expected the invoice to follow. At the nine studios that shared numbers for this story, total delivery spend rose between 6 and 24 percent over the same period.

The gap is volume and concurrency. A cam platform streaming 400 simultaneous sessions at 720p, with an archive and a preview tier, generates four times the egress per viewer that a membership site did in 2023. Cheaper gigabytes met more gigabytes and lost.

Where the bill actually moved

Three lines: storage for the archive, which grows and is almost never pruned; transcoding for the long tail of device profiles, which doubles every time a new format is launched; and the origin shielding that keeps a live event from taking the edge down. None of those were on the 2023 spreadsheet.

“We negotiated a 30 percent rate cut and our bill went up 18 percent. Both numbers are true.”Infrastructure lead at a membership platform

The studios that cut spend did it by policy rather than by contract: archive retention of 90 days instead of indefinite, two output profiles instead of five, and live events pinned to a single region with an origin shield. One studio cut delivery spend 21 percent in a quarter without changing vendors.

The lesson is unglamorous. Delivery is a demand problem dressed as a pricing problem, and a rate cut only helps a studio that already knows what it is delivering, to whom, and how often.

Key takeaways

  • Per-gigabyte pricing fell about 40 percent while total delivery spend rose 6–24 percent.
  • Archive storage, profile proliferation and origin shielding are the lines that grew.
  • Retention caps and fewer output profiles cut spend faster than renegotiating rates.
