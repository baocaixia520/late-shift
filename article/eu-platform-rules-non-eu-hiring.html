Platform Rules Reshape How Non-EU Studios Hire

A platform operating in Europe now has to know who its business partners are. Studios outside the EU are discovering that “knowing” means paperwork they have never been asked for.

By R. Feld / 29 October 2026 / 2 min read

Platform rules that require traceability of business partners have started to reach studios outside the EU. A US studio that contracts performers who work from three countries is now being asked for identity verification, contracting entity details and a statement of where the work is performed.

The rule is aimed at the platform, not the studio. But the platform can only comply by passing the requirement down the chain, and the chain ends at a studio that has never had to document a contractor's jurisdiction before.

What the questionnaire asks

Legal entity and registration number. Country of tax residence for each contractor. The jurisdiction where services are performed. Whether the contractor is an individual or a company. And, in two cases, a declaration that the contractor is not subject to an exclusion the platform is required to check.

“We had to ask twenty-two people where they actually work. Four of them were surprised.”Studio operator who completed the questionnaire

The operational cost is bigger than the paperwork. A studio that has been paying contractors through a single account now needs a record per contractor, and a studio that has been treating everyone as self-employed needs a lawyer to confirm that the classification holds in each jurisdiction.

The upside, if there is one, is that the documentation makes financing easier. Two studios said the same records they assembled for a platform review were accepted by a lender within a month, because a lender asks similar questions and rarely gets a clean answer.

Key takeaways

  • Platform traceability rules are being passed down to non-EU studios as questionnaires.
  • The work is contractor-level: jurisdiction, entity type and tax residence for each person.
  • The same records shortened two studios' later financing reviews.
