DTC or Marketplace? The Storefront Question

The same product sells for $2 more on a marketplace and nets $3 less. The difference is the customer list, and whether the brand is allowed to keep it.

By June Alcaraz / 29 September 2026 / 2 min read

A $14 product sold direct carries about $5.60 of contribution after payment fees and shipping. The same product on a marketplace at $16 carries $2.60, because the marketplace takes 15 percent, the fulfilment fee is higher and the brand pays for promoted placement to be seen at all.

The gap is the customer list, and most marketplaces will not give it to you. A brand that never sees the buyer cannot remarket, cannot build a subscription, and cannot tell whether a repeat purchase came from the product or from the platform's recommendation engine.

When the marketplace wins anyway

Discovery. A new brand with no audience gets its first 200 orders on a marketplace in the time it would take to build a storefront that converts. Three brands said the marketplace is where they test a product and the storefront is where they scale it, which is a rational division of labour and not a compromise.

“The marketplace is a sampling channel that happens to pay for itself.”Founder of a two-year-old novelty brand

The cost of running both is the operations. Two catalogues, two return policies, two stock pools and a reconciliation that has to happen weekly or the stock numbers diverge. Brands that run both successfully keep one inventory system and treat the marketplace as a sales channel rather than a second shop.

The decision that actually matters is pricing. A brand that undercuts its own storefront on a marketplace trains the buyer to wait for a promotion, and the storefront then carries the discount without the volume. The brands holding price on both channels describe the marketplace as expensive distribution rather than cheap acquisition, which is the more accurate framing.

Key takeaways

  • A $14 direct sale contributes about $5.60; the same product at $16 on a marketplace contributes about $2.60.
  • The real cost is the customer list, which marketplaces rarely hand over.
  • Marketplaces are a sampling channel; storefronts are where repeat revenue lives.
