Awards Campaigns Move In-House as Studios Cut PR Retainers

The five-figure awards retainer is being replaced by one staff publicist, a spreadsheet and a calendar. Trade categories were the first line to move.

By Marla Vance / 30 October 2026 / 2 min read

Three studios that used to keep an outside publicist on retainer from September to February have moved the work to a salaried staffer this season. The retainer ran $4,500 to $9,000 a month. The staffer costs less, works all year and, crucially, answers the phone in the same building as the people who have to approve the submission.

Awards work in this trade is less about glamour than logistics. Each category has an entry window, a fee, an eligibility rule about release dates and a set of materials that must be identical across submissions. A studio chasing six categories in four shows is managing roughly forty deadlines, and missing one costs the entry fee and the year.

What the retainer actually bought

Outside publicists brought relationships with the shows, a template library and a second pair of eyes on eligibility. Studios that insourced kept the templates and lost the relationships, which is why two of the three still pay a per-submission fee to a consultant who knows the category chairs.

“The retainer was a subscription to somebody else's calendar. We bought the calendar.”Studio marketing director

The cost of entry has risen regardless. Fees are up between 8 and 15 percent this season, and two shows added a late-window surcharge. Studios that budget awards at a flat $20,000 are now running closer to $27,000 once travel and the after-party table are counted.

What changed in the copy is subtler. In-house teams write for the category they are entering, not for the show's audience, and submissions have become noticeably more technical. One jury member said the entries now read like product documentation with a plot summary attached, which he described as an improvement.

Key takeaways

  • Awards retainers of $4,500–$9,000 a month are being replaced by salaried staff plus per-submission consultants.
  • Entry fees are up 8–15 percent this season, with late-window surcharges at two shows.
  • In-house campaigns optimise for eligibility rules, which is where entries are actually lost.
