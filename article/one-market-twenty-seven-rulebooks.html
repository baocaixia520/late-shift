One Market, Twenty-Seven Rulebooks

The directive is a single document. The obligations are twenty-seven national implementations, and no two of them define reporting the same way.

By R. Feld / 7 November 2026 / 2 min read

A single-market directive has produced twenty-seven national implementations, and the differences are not cosmetic. Reporting frequency ranges from quarterly to annual, the definition of a “significant incident” varies by threshold, and four member states require notification in the local language within 72 hours.

For an operator serving the whole market, that is a compliance matrix with twenty-seven columns and no shared form. Two companies that built the matrix this year said the work took four months and cost €60,000 to €140,000, most of it legal review rather than systems.

The four variables that change the work

Who must be notified — a regulator, a designated body, or both. What triggers notification — a threshold, a category, or a complaint. How quickly — 24, 48 or 72 hours. And in what language, which is where most missed deadlines originate, because the clock starts before translation is arranged.

“We were compliant in eleven countries and late in three. The three were the ones with a language requirement.”Compliance director at a platform operating in the EU

The practical answer is a template library rather than a central policy: one incident report template per member state, pre-translated, with the notification address and the deadline printed at the top. Two operators now keep the templates in the same system as the incident log, so the report is generated rather than written.

The larger point is that harmonisation has not arrived. The directive harmonised the principle and left the procedure to national regulators, which means the compliance cost scales with the number of markets rather than the size of the business.

Key takeaways

  • One directive, 27 implementations, with reporting frequency ranging from quarterly to annual.
  • Building the compliance matrix cost €60,000–€140,000 at two companies.
  • Pre-translated templates per member state are the practical fix; language is where deadlines are missed.
