Search Is Fragmenting. Publishers Need a Second Door

Referral traffic from search fell 22 percent year on year at four sites. The replacements — a newsletter, an app and a paid channel — each convert differently, and none of them is free.

By Tobias Lund / 23 October 2026 / 2 min read

Search referrals to four adult sites fell between 14 and 31 percent year on year, according to analytics shared by their operators. The pattern is familiar: fewer result slots, more summarised answers, and a higher bar for the kind of page that gets surfaced at all.

The replacements are not equivalent. Email converts at 4 to 9 percent for a cold list and 18 to 25 percent for a list built from existing customers. An app install converts at 2 to 5 percent and then retains worse than a bookmark. Paid search costs $0.40 to $1.80 a click in this sector and is rising.

What a second door costs

A newsletter needs a sending platform, a template, a list and someone to write it weekly; the real cost is the writing. An app needs maintenance and store policy compliance, which for this category can mean removal at any time. A paid channel needs a budget that scales with volume, which is the opposite of a moat.

“Search was a tenant, not an asset. We were renting traffic and calling it audience.”Publisher who rebuilt around email

The sites that held traffic did two things: they made the destination worth returning to, and they captured the address before the visit ended. A membership site with a login has an advantage here that a free tube site does not, because the login is the second door.

The tactical version is less romantic. Add an email capture to the second page of the session, put the archive behind a soft registration, and stop treating social referrals as a channel that will still exist in three years.

Key takeaways

  • Search referrals fell 14–31 percent year on year across four sites.
  • Email converts at 4–9 percent cold and 18–25 percent on an owned list; paid search costs $0.40–$1.80 a click.
  • A login or a captured address is the only traffic that survives an algorithm change.
