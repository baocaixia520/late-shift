Brussels Moves on Age Verification: What the Draft Text Does

A committee draft would make age assurance a platform obligation rather than a member-state option. The compliance timeline is tighter than the consultation suggests.

By R. Feld / 12 November 2026 / 2 min read

A committee draft circulating in Brussels would move age assurance from a national option to a platform-level obligation, with a compliance date eighteen months after entry into force. The text is 42 pages, and the operative language sits in a single paragraph that defines who is responsible when a third-party vendor fails.

The draft keeps the vendor model but shifts liability. A platform that uses a certified provider is presumed compliant unless it had reason to doubt the provider's performance; a platform that builds its own is assessed directly. That asymmetry is deliberate, and it is the reason three large operators have already started certification conversations.

The timeline in the text

Publication of the final text, then six months for the certification framework, then twelve months for platforms to comply. Operators reading the annex say the certification framework is the bottleneck, not the platform work, because no certification body has yet been designated in most member states.

“Everyone is planning for the compliance date. Nobody is planning for the six months before it.”Policy consultant following the committee

The draft also touches retention. Refusal records must be kept for twelve months, verification records for six, and neither may be retained beyond that without a documented legal basis. Operators that currently keep everything indefinitely will need a deletion schedule, which is a systems change rather than a policy change.

What is not in the text matters as much. There is no harmonised definition of “reasonable steps”, no fee cap on certification, and no mutual recognition clause, which means a platform serving five member states may face five assessments.

Key takeaways

  • The draft makes age assurance a platform obligation with an 18-month compliance window.
  • Using a certified vendor creates a presumption of compliance; building in-house does not.
  • Retention is specified: 12 months for refusals, six for verifications, with no mutual recognition.
