In-Game Payments Are a Regulatory Problem Now

Virtual currency is being treated as a consumer product, not a design choice. Two storefronts changed their disclosure rules this quarter and studios are rewriting their shops.

By June Alcaraz / 26 October 2026 / 2 min read

Two storefronts tightened their rules for in-game currency this quarter. Both now require the real-money equivalent to be shown at the point of purchase, and one requires the currency pack to disclose the per-unit price. Studios that sell three pack sizes with a bonus tier are rewriting their shop screens.

The regulatory logic is consumer protection, not content. A pack priced at $9.99 that grants 1,200 coins plus 200 bonus coins is a discount in disguise, and regulators in two markets have started treating undisclosed bonus structures as a pricing violation.

What changes in the shop

The headline price has to be the real one. A studio that used to advertise 1,200 coins now advertises $9.99 and 1,200 coins. The bonus tier either disappears or becomes a visible discount off a stated unit price, which makes the ladder legible and also makes it harder to hide a bad exchange rate.

“We were selling arithmetic. Now we have to show the arithmetic.”Monetisation lead at a small studio

Refund exposure is the part that hurts. A disclosed price makes it easier for a player to argue that a purchase was mispriced, and two studios reported refund requests up 15 to 20 percent in the first month after the change. The requests are mostly small and mostly correct.

The studios that adapted quickly did it by simplifying: two packs instead of four, one currency instead of two, and a receipt that states what was bought in the same units the shop used. It is less elegant and considerably easier to defend.

Key takeaways

  • Two storefronts now require the real-money equivalent at the point of purchase.
  • Bonus coin packs are being read as undisclosed discounts in two markets.
  • Refund requests rose 15–20 percent at studios that disclosed their pricing.
